Course Syllabus — Winter Session 2025
========================================

STAT 350 is offered in the Winter Session as a single **intensive asynchronous online** section
running **December 22, 2025 – January 9, 2026**. There are no scheduled class meetings: you work
through the lecture videos, readings, and assignments on your own, paced by daily deadlines. The
session covers the same thirteen chapters as the 16-week course and uses the same webbook and
video resources, but compresses them into eleven days and delivers both exams online through
Respondus LockDown Browser.

.. admonition:: Your Official Syllabus
   :class: important

   The official syllabus for your section is available through Brightspace and governs in case
   of any discrepancy.

.. admonition:: This Is a Full Semester in Eleven Days
   :class: important

   Treat the Winter Session like a full-time internship: plan on **at least eight focused hours
   every instructional day**. There are **no makeup quizzes or exams** and **no late homework**.
   Quizzes lock at midnight Eastern and are not reopened. If you fall behind at any point, you
   will probably need to drop the course.

Course Description & Prerequisites
-----------------------------------

*(Credit Hours: 3.00)* A data-oriented introduction to the fundamental concepts and methods of
applied statistics. Exploratory analysis of data. Sample design and experimental design.
Probability distributions and simulation. Sampling distributions. The reasoning of statistical
inference. Confidence intervals and tests for one and two samples. Inference for regression and
correlation. Essential use is made of statistical software throughout.

**Prerequisites:** Calculus 2. You must demonstrate proficiency in basic derivative and integral
calculus in both homework and exams. More advanced topics may be introduced, but assessment will
not exceed a Calculus 2 level of difficulty.

**Daily quizzes (10%)**

Timed 20-minute quizzes in Brightspace, one per instructional day, covering material from recent
chapters — not only the current day's. Each quiz is worth 10 points and mixes true/false questions (1 point each)
with multiple-choice questions (2 points each), and mirrors the more theoretical questions you
will see on the proctored exams. One attempt only; quizzes lock at midnight Eastern. The **two
lowest quiz scores are dropped**, and there are **no make-up quizzes**.

**Homework and computer assignments (34%)**

Delivered through Edfinity, due the day after the corresponding reading, before midnight. Due
dates are marked on both Brightspace and Edfinity. Homework problems are automatically graded:
you get **10 attempts** on free-response problems and **3** on multiple choice — use them to
learn, not to guess. Computer assignments use a provided data set and require R code, graphs, and
written interpretation. The **lowest homework score and the lowest computer assignment score are
dropped**. **No late submissions are accepted**, because of the accelerated pace of the session.
The syllabus assignment is on Brightspace rather than Edfinity.

**Exams (56%)**

One midterm (24%) and a comprehensive final (32%) — see the next section for dates, windows, and
requirements.

**Course evaluation (+1% bonus)**

The anonymous online evaluation is open **Tuesday, January 6 through Friday, January 9, 2026**;
you will receive an email from Instructional Data Processing at your Purdue address with a direct
link. If at least **80% of enrolled students** complete it by January 9, every student receives
**1% extra credit** added to their final course percentage. Results are released only after final
grades are submitted.

Exams
-----------------------------------

Both exams are delivered online in Brightspace and proctored with **Respondus LockDown Browser**
with webcam monitoring.

   * - **Exam**

     - 24 hours on day 6 — Wednesday, December 31

   * - Final Exam
     - 24 hours on day 11 — Friday, January 9

- Each exam is **one attempt only** — you cannot pause, exit, and restart. The exam submits
  automatically when time expires.
- Exams are **closed book but open note**: your own handwritten or printed notes are allowed.
  Condense them into crib sheets. Tablets and iPads may not be used during the exam — not as your
  notes and not as a device — and neither may phones, smartwatches, or other electronics.
- **No formulas will be provided.** If a statistical table is needed, its URL will be allowed by
  the LockDown Browser, but printing a physical copy is strongly recommended.
- A calculator is strongly recommended, though the LockDown Browser includes a scientific
  calculator.
- The final exam is comprehensive, weighted roughly **70% toward material after the midterm** and
  **30% toward earlier material**.
- **Requirements:** a desktop or laptop running Windows or macOS (tablets are not supported), a
  2 Mbps or better internet connection, a government-issued photo ID for identity verification
  before the exam starts, a working microphone, and a camera positioned to show
  both your face and your writing area — most built-in laptop webcams are not wide enough, so an
  external camera may be required. Keep your face and desk in view, with good lighting and no
  obstructions, for the entire exam.
- **Do a test run.** Use the practice quiz in Brightspace beforehand, on the same device and
  network you will use for the exam, and watch the setup video linked on the
  :ref:`Winter Session home page <winter_index>`.

See :ref:`exams-index-winter` for exam-by-exam procedures and preparation materials.

.. admonition:: No Make-Up Exams

How to Succeed in This Course
-----------------------------------

Each instructional day follows the same four steps:

1. **Absorb (≈4 hours).** Complete the assigned reading in the webbook and watch the segmented
   lecture videos. Use the search bar and timeline in the video viewer to target topics you find
   challenging.
2. **Apply (≈4 hours).** Work the Edfinity homework and computer assignments on scratch paper
   first, then submit. Record the steps that slow you down and revisit those notes before the
   exams.
3. **Assess (≈20 minutes).** Take the timed daily quiz in Brightspace.
4. **Review (≈1 hour).** Check posted solutions, revisit video segments on hard topics, and post
   or answer questions on the peer board.

A few habits that make the difference:

- Check Brightspace announcements and your Purdue email every morning — due dates and course
  changes are posted there.
- Keep tidy notes as you go. The exams are open-note, and notes written the night before are not
  as useful as notes written while you learned the material.
- Start early. The course opens before December 22, so begin working ahead.
- Lean on the peer discussion board. In a session this compressed, your classmates are your
  fastest source of help; instructor replies typically come in the late afternoon on weekdays, and weekend
  replies may take longer than 24 hours.

Academic Integrity & AI Policy
-----------------------------------

You are expected to uphold the `Purdue Honor Pledge
<https://www.purdue.edu/odos/osrr/honor-pledge/about.html>`_: "As a boilermaker pursuing academic
excellence, I pledge to be honest and true in all that I do. Accountable together — we are
Purdue."

Cheating on an exam results in an **F in the course** — this includes communicating exam details
to students who have not yet taken it and using non-approved materials during the exam. Cheating
on quizzes, homework, and computer assignments results in a zero for that assignment. Incidents
are handled by the course coordinator and referred to `OSRR
<https://www.purdue.edu/odos/osrr/>`_ for review at the university level. Uploading or sharing
STAT 350 course materials to any website, copying previously posted keys (including keys found on
the internet), and using services like Chegg, Course Hero, or Quizlet to obtain answers are all
cheating. Suspected breaches can be reported to integrity@purdue.edu or 765-494-8778 (anonymous
submissions accepted).

Because the exams are webcam-proctored, the monitoring system flags behaviors such as frequently
looking away from the screen, talking or moving your lips, leaving the camera view, another
person appearing in the room, unusual background noise, and use of unauthorized materials. All
recordings are reviewed.

**Good collaboration** — we encourage working together the right way:

- Begin each assignment on your own before discussing the hard parts with others.
- Discuss ideas and approaches for conceptual clarity, and compare answers, but finish each
  assignment yourself so your submission reflects your own understanding.
- Use the peer board to ask conceptual questions, share strategies, and point to relevant
  sections of the webbook. Describe your approach and ask for guidance rather than answers.
- If you need help, ask a TA — but still do the actual problem yourself.

**Academic misconduct** — the following are cheating, and both the student who copies and the
student who allows copying are penalized:

- Dividing the assignment among a group and swapping finished answers.
- Posting answers, worked solutions, full code, screenshots of problems, or assessment content on
  the Brightspace discussion board or any public forum.
- Soliciting or providing step-by-step solutions on external sites or group chats, paid or free.
- Attending a group work session without having first attempted the problems yourself.
- Letting others copy your work, freeloading on group efforts, or submitting work produced by
  someone else or by AI as your own.
- Having a tutor, TA, or tutoring service (Chegg, Course Hero, etc.) work your problems,
  including referring to electronically posted materials.
- Uploading any STAT 350 materials to external sites or sharing them beyond the course.

.. admonition:: AI Usage Policy
   :class: note

   AI tools (such as Anthropic's Claude, OpenAI's ChatGPT, or other large language models) may be
   used to **check your reasoning, get hints, understand concepts, debug R code, explore
   alternative approaches to problems you have already solved, or generate practice
   problems — after you have attempted the work yourself**. They may **not** be used to generate
   complete solutions, to submit AI-generated code or text as your own work, to complete
   assignments without your own substantial effort, or during exams. The course-specific tools
   **Ask Anna** (within Edfinity) and the **AI Course Assistant** fall under the same permitted
   and prohibited uses; in particular, neither is permitted during exams. You are responsible for
   being able to explain
   any work you submit; homework is practice for exams where AI is not available. Violations of
   this policy are treated as academic misconduct and reported to OSRR.

Learning Remotely
-----------------------------------

This is a fully online course, and we want the learning environment to be a safe one. All
opinions and experiences must be respected in the tolerant spirit of academic discourse. You are
encouraged to comment on, question, or critique an idea, but you may not attack an individual.
Sarcasm and humor are easily misread online, so a few guidelines for discussion boards:

- Watch how much space you take up in a discussion and leave room for other students.
- Do not use offensive language, vernacular, or slang that could be misinterpreted, and do not
  type in all capitals — it reads as shouting.
- Keep an open mind and be willing to express a minority opinion.
- Think and edit before you send, and take in feedback from others.
