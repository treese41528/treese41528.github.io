Course Syllabus — Fall 2026
========================================

STAT 350 is offered in three instructional modalities: **Traditional Lecture (In-Person)**,
**Flipped**, and **Asynchronous Online** — with traditional lecture sections on both the
West Lafayette and Indianapolis campuses. All modalities cover the same material, use the
same webbook and video resources, and take the same in-person evening exams. They differ in
how class time (if any) is structured and how participation, quizzes, and homework are
weighted.

This section summarizes the general syllabus shared by all sections, and one page per modality
covers the details specific to that format.

   official syllabus for *your* section is available through Brightspace and governs in case
   of any discrepancy.

Course Description & Prerequisites
-----------------------------------

*(Credit Hours: 3.00)* A data-oriented introduction to the fundamental concepts and methods of
applied statistics. Exploratory analysis of data. Sample design and experimental design.
Probability distributions and simulation. Sampling distributions. The reasoning of statistical
inference. Confidence intervals and tests for one and two samples. Inference for regression
and correlation. Essential use is made of statistical software throughout.

**Prerequisites:** Calc II (C– or better) or equivalent — e.g., Purdue MA 16200/16600/17300/18100,
MA 16020–16021, MA 16400/16900, or MA 22200. You will need to perform integrations in both
homework and exams, but assessment will not extend beyond a Calculus 2 level of difficulty.

For the chapter-by-chapter roadmap, see the :ref:`Course Introduction & Overview <course_intro>`.

-----------------------------------

All sections — **including Asynchronous Online** — take proctored paper exams in person on campus.

     - Finals week — date/time/location set by the Registrar

- Exams are closed-book. Statistical tables are provided when needed; **formulas are not**.
- Bring pens, pencils, erasers, and a calculator. Room assignments are posted in Brightspace.
- The final exam is a comprehensive two-hour assessment: roughly 60–70% on material after
  Exam 2, and 30–40% revisiting earlier material.
- Graded exams are returned through Gradescope. Regrade requests must be submitted on
  Gradescope **before the last day of class**.
- Class days are cancelled to compensate for the evening exams — the exact dates depend on
  your section and are marked on your section's course schedule (linked from each modality
  page).

**Makeup exams.** Valid reasons include Purdue-required activities, direct exam conflicts,
bereavement, and medical grounds. For non-emergencies, submit the Make-Up Exam Form on
Brightspace at least **one week before** the exam. For emergencies, email your instructor with
documentation before **9 AM the day after** the exam. Missing these deadlines may result in a
score of 0. See :ref:`exams-index` for procedures and preparation materials.

Academic Integrity & AI Policy
-----------------------------------

You are expected to uphold the `Purdue Honor Pledge <https://www.purdue.edu/odos/osrr/honor-pledge/about.html>`_.
Cheating on an exam results in an **F in the course** — this includes communicating exam
details to students who have not yet taken it and using non-approved materials during the
exam. Cheating on assignments results in a zero, and all incidents are referred to the Office
of Student Rights and Responsibilities; additional penalties, up to removal from the
university, may be considered. Uploading or sharing STAT 350 course materials to any website,
copying previously posted keys (including keys found on the internet), and using services
like Chegg, Course Hero, or Quizlet to obtain answers are all cheating.

Suspected breaches can be reported to integrity@purdue.edu or 765-494-8778 (anonymous
submissions accepted). Incidents are handled by the course coordinator and referred to
`OSRR <https://www.purdue.edu/odos/osrr/>`_ (765-494-1250, osrr@purdue.edu).

**Good collaboration** — we encourage working together the right way:

- Start by working every homework problem on your own, then form a small group with others
  who have also attempted every problem.
- Discuss ideas and approaches for the harder problems, but finish the homework yourself so
  your submission reflects your own understanding.
- For review problems, work individually first, then use the group to discuss concepts and
  practice problems.
- Take turns explaining concepts and practice problems to each other.
- If you need help, ask a TA or tutor — but still do the actual problem yourself.

**Academic misconduct** — the following are cheating, and both the student who copies and the
student who allows copying are penalized:

- Dividing up the problems among a group and sharing the work.
- Posting answers to a homework question publicly, or reading someone else's answers before
  completing your own work.
- Attending or joining group work without having first attempted every problem yourself, or
  copying a partner's answers.
- Starting a problem yourself but copying someone else's solution after getting stuck.
- Having a tutor, TA, or tutoring service (Chegg, Course Hero, etc.) work your problems,
  including referring to electronically posted materials.
- Sharing a computer assignment, printing two copies of the same output, or doing the
  assignment together on one computer.
- Not keeping your exam covered.

.. admonition:: AI Usage Policy
   :class: note

   AI tools (such as Anthropic's Claude, OpenAI's ChatGPT, or other large language models) may
   be used to **check your reasoning, get hints, understand concepts, debug R code, explore
   alternative approaches to problems you have already solved, or generate practice
   problems — after you have attempted the work yourself**. They may **not** be used to
   generate complete solutions, to submit AI-generated code or text as your own work, to
   complete assignments without your own substantial effort, or during exams. You are
   responsible for being able to explain any work you submit; homework is practice for exams
   where AI is not available. Violations of this policy are treated as academic misconduct
   and reported to OSRR.

Attendance & Absences
-----------------------------------

This course follows Purdue's Academic Regulations on attendance and the Office of the Dean of
Students' class-absence policies (posted in Brightspace under **University Policies and
Statements**). You are expected to be present for every meeting of the classes in which you
are enrolled; in sections with class meetings, attendance is taken at the beginning of each
class. When conflicts or absences can be anticipated — University-sponsored activities,
religious observations — inform your instructor as far in advance as possible. For
unanticipated or emergency absences, contact your instructor as soon as possible by email or
a private Brightspace post. For cases that fall under excused-absence regulations, you or
your representative should contact your instructor or complete the appropriate notification
forms on the Office of the Dean of Students (ODOS) website; ODOS may grant excused absences
for grief/bereavement, military service, jury duty, parenting leave, or emergent medical
care.

Section Changes
-----------------------------------

There is no "section change" option at Purdue — you must drop your old section and add the
new one. **Confirm you can add the new section before dropping the old one.** You may
temporarily lose access to Edfinity and Scholar during the change, and if you are changing
instructors, contact your old instructor *before* dropping so your grade information can be
transferred — it is your responsibility if any information is lost. Observe the add/drop
deadlines for cancelling without a transcript record.

Accessibility & Accommodations
-----------------------------------

Purdue strives to make learning experiences as accessible as possible. If you anticipate or
experience physical or academic barriers based on disability, you are welcome to discuss
options with your instructor, and you are encouraged to contact the **Disability Resource
Center** at drc@purdue.edu or 765-494-1247. If the DRC has determined reasonable
accommodations, `release your Course Accommodation Letter
<https://www.purdue.edu/drc/students/course-accommodation-letter.php>`_ to your instructor
and discuss implementation as early as possible.

**Accommodated testing.** If your accommodations include test accommodations, you must
schedule each exam in advance:

- **West Lafayette:** schedule with `Purdue Testing Services
  <https://www.purdue.edu/studentsuccess/testing-services/accommodated-testing/student.php>`_
  at least **96 hours (four days)** before the exam date; for finals week, by the Friday
  before quiet period.
- **Indianapolis:** schedule through your **DRC Student Portal** at least **two business
  days** before the exam date; for finals week, by the Friday before quiet period.

Inform your instructor immediately if testing services are at capacity so other arrangements
can be made. Missing these deadlines risks not having your accommodations for that exam.

  Student Life website.

  offices in West Lafayette or Indianapolis.
