Course Syllabus — Summer Session 2026
========================================

STAT 35000 is offered in the Summer Session as a single **asynchronous online** section running
**June 15 – August 7, 2026** (the second eight weeks). There are no scheduled class meetings: you
work through the lecture videos, readings, and assignments on your own, paced by weekly
deadlines. The session covers the same thirteen chapters as the 16-week course and uses the same
webbook and video resources, but compresses them into eight weeks and delivers all three exams
online through Respondus LockDown Browser.

.. admonition:: Your Official Syllabus
   :class: important

   The official syllabus for your section is available through Brightspace and governs in case
   of any discrepancy.

.. admonition:: A Full Semester in Eight Weeks
   :class: important

   Expect **15–20 focused hours per week**, with some weeks considerably heavier than others.
   Video time alone ranges from 2 to 5+ hours depending on the week. The compressed calendar
   leaves little room for catching up, so use the lighter weeks to build a buffer for the heavy
   ones.

Course Description & Prerequisites
-----------------------------------

*(Credit Hours: 3.00)* A data-oriented introduction to the fundamental concepts and methods of
applied statistics. Exploratory analysis of data. Sample design and experimental design.
Probability distributions and simulation. Sampling distributions. The reasoning of statistical
inference. Confidence intervals and tests for one and two samples. Inference for regression and
correlation. Essential use is made of statistical software throughout.

**Prerequisites:** Calculus 2. You must demonstrate proficiency in basic derivative and integral
calculus in both homework and exams. More advanced topics may be introduced, but assessment will
not exceed a Calculus 2 level of difficulty.

**Weekly quizzes (10%)**

Timed quizzes in Brightspace covering that week's material. Each quiz is worth 10 points and
mixes true/false questions (1 point each) with multiple-choice questions (2 points each), and
mirrors the more theoretical questions you will see on the proctored exams. The **two lowest quiz
scores are dropped**, and there are **no make-up quizzes**.

**Homework and computer assignments (26%)**

Delivered through Edfinity, due the day after the corresponding reading, before midnight. Due
dates are marked on both Brightspace and Edfinity. Homework problems are automatically graded:
you get **10 attempts** on free-response problems and **3** on multiple choice — use them to
learn, not to guess. Computer assignments use a provided data set and require R code, graphs, and
written interpretation. The **lowest homework score and the lowest computer assignment score are
dropped**. A **two-day late submission window** is available for assignments. The syllabus
assignment is on Brightspace rather than Edfinity.

**Exams (64%)**

Two exams (18% each) and a comprehensive final (28%) — see the next section for dates, windows,
and requirements.

**Course evaluation (+1% bonus)**

The anonymous online evaluation is open **Monday, July 27 through Friday, August 7, 2026**; you
will receive an email from Instructional Data Processing at your Purdue address with a direct
link. If at least **80% of enrolled students** complete it by August 7, every student receives
**1% extra credit** added to their final course percentage. Results are released only after final
grades are submitted.

-----------------------------------

All three exams are delivered online in Brightspace and proctored with **Respondus LockDown

- Each exam is **one attempt only** — you cannot pause, exit, and restart. The exam submits
  automatically when time expires.
- Exams are **closed book but open note**: your own handwritten or printed notes are allowed.
  Condense them into crib sheets.
- **No formulas will be provided.** If a statistical table is needed, its URL will be allowed by
  the LockDown Browser, but printing a physical copy is strongly recommended.
- A calculator is strongly recommended, though the LockDown Browser includes a scientific
  calculator.
- The final exam is comprehensive, weighted roughly **60% toward material after Exam 2** and
  **40% toward material from Exams 1 and 2**.
- **Requirements:** a desktop or laptop running Windows or macOS (tablets are not supported), a
  stable 2 Mbps or better internet connection, a working microphone, and a camera positioned to
  show both your face and your writing area — most built-in laptop webcams are not wide enough,
  so an external camera, or an external mouse and keyboard, may be required. Keep your face and
  desk in view, with good lighting and no obstructions, for the entire exam.
- **Do a test run.** Use the practice quiz in Brightspace beforehand, on the same device and
  network you will use for the exam, and watch the setup video on the course website.

Attendance & Engagement
-----------------------------------

Because this section is asynchronous and online, there are no scheduled class meetings and
attendance is not graded. **Engagement, however, is required.** Engagement is demonstrated by
on-time completion of Edfinity homework, computer assignments, Brightspace quizzes, the syllabus
assignment, and the exams. Simply logging into Brightspace does not constitute engagement for
federal financial aid or for tracking purposes. Students who do not engage in academically
related activities by the published deadlines may have their financial aid eligibility reviewed
by the University, regardless of any later make-up arrangements.

Excused absences are handled through the Office of the Dean of Students (ODOS) process and are
granted for grief/bereavement, military service, jury duty, parenting leave, and certain types of
medical care. For anticipated conflicts such as religious observance or University-sponsored
activities, notify the instructor as far in advance as possible.

How to Succeed in This Course
-----------------------------------

Each week follows the same four steps:

1. **Absorb (≈6–8 hours).** Complete the assigned reading in the webbook and watch the segmented
   lecture videos. Use the search bar and timeline in the video viewer to target topics you find
   challenging.
2. **Apply (≈6–8 hours).** Work the Edfinity homework on scratch paper first, then submit. Review
   the computer assignment tutorials before attempting the assignments.
3. **Assess (≈20 minutes).** Take the timed weekly quiz in Brightspace.
4. **Review (≈2 hours).** Check posted solutions, revisit video segments on hard topics, and post
   or answer questions on the peer board.

A few habits that make the difference:

- Check Brightspace weekly for deadlines and announcements. The compressed calendar leaves little
  room for catching up.
- Work ahead during the lighter weeks to build a buffer for the heavier ones — the workload is
  not uniform.
- Keep tidy notes as you go. The exams are open-note, and notes written the night before are not
  as useful as notes written while you learned the material.
- Lean on the peer discussion board. Your classmates are your fastest source of help; instructor replies typically come in the late afternoon on
  weekdays, and weekend replies may take longer than 24 hours.

Academic Integrity & AI Policy
-----------------------------------

You are expected to uphold the `Purdue Honor Pledge
<https://www.purdue.edu/odos/osrr/honor-pledge/about.html>`_: "As a boilermaker pursuing academic
excellence, I pledge to be honest and true in all that I do. Accountable together — we are
Purdue."

Cheating on an exam results in an **F in the course** — this includes communicating exam details
to students who have not yet taken it and using non-approved materials during the exam. Cheating
on homework, computer assignments, and quizzes results in a zero for that assignment. All
incidents are addressed by the course coordinator and referred to `OSRR
<https://www.purdue.edu/odos/osrr/>`_ (765-494-1250, osrr@purdue.edu) for review at the
university level; additional penalties, up to removal from the university, may be considered.
Copying previously posted keys (including keys found on the internet) and using services like
Chegg, Course Hero, or Quizlet to obtain answers are cheating. Suspected breaches can be reported
to integrity@purdue.edu or 765-494-8778 (anonymous submissions accepted).

**Good collaboration** — we encourage working together the right way:

- Start by working every homework problem on your own, then form a small group with others who
  have also attempted every problem.
- Discuss ideas and approaches for the harder problems, but finish the homework yourself so your
  submission reflects your own understanding.
- For review problems, work individually first, then use the group to discuss concepts and
  practice problems.
- Take turns explaining concepts and practice problems to each other.
- If you need help, ask a TA or tutor — but still do the actual problem yourself.

**Academic misconduct** — the following are cheating, and both the student who copies and the
student who allows copying are penalized:

- Screen-sharing during an exam, allowing another person to view your screen or be present within
  range of your camera or microphone, or being in the same physical space as another exam-taker.
- Dividing up the problems among a group and sharing the work.
- Posting answers to a homework question publicly on the Brightspace discussion board.
- Attending or joining group work without having first attempted every problem yourself, copying
  a partner's answers, or letting an unprepared partner copy yours.
- Starting a problem yourself but copying someone else's solution after getting stuck, or reading
  someone else's answers before completing your own work.
- Having a tutor, TA, or tutoring service (Chegg, Course Hero, etc.) work your problems,
  including referring to electronically posted materials.
- Sharing a computer assignment, printing two copies of the same output, or doing the assignment
  together on one computer.
- Not keeping your exam covered.

.. admonition:: AI Usage Policy
   :class: note

   AI tools (such as Anthropic's Claude, OpenAI's ChatGPT, or other large language models) may be
   used to **check your reasoning, get hints, understand concepts, debug R code, explore
   alternative approaches to problems you have already solved, or generate practice
   problems — after you have attempted the work yourself**. They may **not** be used to generate
   complete solutions, to submit AI-generated code or text as your own work, to complete
   assignments without your own substantial effort, or during exams. The course-specific tools
   **Ask Anna** (within Edfinity) and **LatticeAI** fall under the same permitted and prohibited
   uses; in particular, neither is permitted during exams.

   You are responsible for being able to explain any work you submit; homework is practice for
   exams where AI is not available. If unauthorized AI use is suspected, you may be asked to
   explain your reasoning verbally or to reproduce comparable work without AI assistance, and an
   inability to do so will be treated as evidence of a violation. This course does not rely on
   automated AI-detection software. Violations of this policy are treated as academic misconduct
   and reported to OSRR.

Accessibility & Accommodations
-----------------------------------

Purdue strives to make learning experiences as accessible as possible. If you anticipate or
experience physical or academic barriers based on disability, you are welcome to discuss options
with your instructor, and you are encouraged to contact the **Disability Resource Center** at
drc@purdue.edu or 765-494-1247. If the DRC has determined reasonable accommodations, `release
your Course Accommodation Letter
<https://www.purdue.edu/drc/students/course-accommodation-letter.php>`_ to your instructor and
discuss implementation as early as possible.

Every member of this course should be able to access, use, and learn from the materials we share,
in accordance with Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act. The instructor is responsible
for ensuring that course materials meet accessibility guidelines; you are responsible for
ensuring that anything you post for others — discussion posts, peer feedback, shared files — is
also accessible.

**Accommodated testing.** Because all three exams are delivered online in Brightspace, there is
nothing to schedule with Purdue Testing Services or the DRC testing office. Release your Course
Accommodation Letter to the instructor and testing accommodations such as **extended time are
applied directly to your Brightspace exams**. Release your letter well before Exam 1 so the
adjustments are in place for every exam.
